Privacy policy

mailq · last updated 24 November 2026

mailq is a command-line program that runs on the user's own computer. It is a personal tool, not a hosted service: there is no mailq server, and its developer receives no data from it.

Data mailq accesses

When you sign in with Google, mailq requests access to your Gmail account (https://mail.google.com/) and your email address (openid, email). It uses this access only to do what you ask on the command line: list folders, search messages, and display message headers and bodies in your terminal. Your email address is used only to check that you signed in with the account named in your mailq configuration.

How data is stored

  • The OAuth refresh token is stored in your operating system's keyring on your computer.
  • Short-lived access tokens are kept in memory only while mailq runs.
  • Messages are displayed in your terminal and are not saved, cached or logged by mailq.

Sharing

mailq does not send your data anywhere except to Google's own servers (for sign-in and IMAP access). It has no analytics, telemetry or advertising, and data is never sold, shared with third parties, or used to train AI models.

Google API Services User Data Policy

mailq's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Removing access

You can revoke mailq's access at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions. This immediately invalidates the stored token. To delete the token from your computer as well, remove the mailq-oauth2 entry from your keyring.
